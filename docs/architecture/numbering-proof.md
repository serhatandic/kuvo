# Work Queue numbers across main-computer changes

**Priority 4 remains partial.** The delegated numbering paths at `0dc01540a` preserve disjoint fresh allocations along one accepted succession history when participants follow the allocator and each takeover inherits the outgoing authority's ceiling. The code does not establish exclusive fresh issuance or disjoint fresh ranges across independently accepted ranked histories. This is a source-level gap. The one-ledger tests exercise local guarantees. No collision was observed.

## One accepted succession history

1. **Role and lease.** In a delegated setup, the accepted setup names the primary and standby by computer key and signing key. The signed leadership certificate identifies the next epoch, predecessor and, on the ranked path, the parent certificate hash when known. The certificate binds the checkpoint by hash. The lease originates in authenticated peer mail. The current standby challenges the primary, which checks the standby's key and the fresh nonce exchange before recording a lease. The 60-second fence checks both wall time and uptime, binding the lease to the setup, epoch, standby key and, for ranked setups, the current certificate hash. A pending leadership proposal also fences fresh grants. The takeover delay and probes measure local reachability. [Leadership and checkpoint verification](../../box/src/link/setup/leadership.ts#L282-L341) · [Checkpoint binding](../../box/src/link/setup/checkpoint.ts#L163-L182) · [Authority mail](../../box/src/link/setup/takeover.ts#L80-L140) · [Lease fence](../../box/src/link/setup/lease.ts#L46-L78)
2. **Allocation and standby acceptance.** Under the number ledger lock, `giveBlock` installs the accepted counter. Under the link lock it checks the current primary role, requester membership, standby and lease again. A fresh block uses the counter and must fit within the epoch's inclusive ceiling. Fresh import claims start at or above the counter and leave room for the block. Older claims can reuse rights already attributed to that requester, with a compatibility path for legacy unattributed grants. The proposal advances `next` past its block, records the request ID and recipient, and stages a signed `queueNumberAllocation` save. The pending proposal supplies no grant response. The named standby checks the primary's sender key and signature, setup ID, epoch and next save sequence. Its signed receipt binds the proposal signature. The primary checks the standby's sender key and signature, its own fence, the sequence and the exact pending signature before installing the accepted value. The documents receiver persists the state under the link lock before sending outgoing receipts. Retrying an ID still in `given` returns its original range without advancing the counter. Entries that record a recipient can only replay to that recipient. Legacy entries can lack a recipient, and the replay table is bounded. 3. **Using a grant.** A member accepts a grant response from its current primary route and only for its outstanding ask ID. `takeBlock` clears the ask and saves the range. `takeNumber` durably removes each consumed number from the local range before returning it. The delegated primary also spends a local block. Already granted numbers can therefore be spent while fresh allocation is fenced. When a computer runs out of locally granted numbers, it can keep the item waiting and ask for a block. 4. **Planned handover.** The primary freezes the checkpoint and leadership proposal under the number and link locks. On the ordinary delegated path it must have a live lease and no pending shared saves. It derives `next` from the accepted ledger and local high-water mark, and reserves a ceiling from that counter. The current standby verifies the exact signed proposal, a counter at least as high as its accepted counter, the same granted ranges and replay entries, and the committed outcomes. The certificate installs the new role and ledger together. A new lease is needed to allocate under the new position. The predecessor's issued blocks are already below the transferred counter, so handing over along this path preserves the separation between those blocks and new ones. 5. **One takeover.** The legacy delegated path accepts the designated standby as successor. The ranked path accepts a takeover signed by an eligible member whose key and signing key match the preceding accepted membership. The checkpointed writer seeds the successor counter with `max(inherited ceiling + 1, accepted ledger next)`. When the inherited ceiling bounds all rights issued by the predecessor, this skips even the unspent part of its reservation. Promotion installs no standby and retains the shared-save requirement. The successor can spend numbers it already holds, but fresh grants wait for a new standby, a lease and an accepted save. When the successor installs a standby, `proposeLeadership` derives the next ceiling from its accepted counter and local high-water mark. ## Where the proof stops

A ranked candidate waits for local timeout and failed reachability probes. Those checks cannot establish that another candidate did not promote, or that the candidate has seen every later reservation certified from its predecessor. The installer accepts a ranked member's own signed takeover of the same predecessor at `epoch + 1`. The parent hash identifies the predecessor, while the epoch increment and successor rank provide neither an exclusive authorization to extend numbering nor a range tied to that successor's identity. The takeover floor guarantees separation from the inherited ceiling only. [Rank and reachability checks](../../box/src/link/setup/takeover.ts#L253-L288) · [Takeover installation](../../box/src/link/setup/leadership.ts#L282-L314)

After promotion, the primary proposes the first standby for its accepted position. The proposed standby can accept the checkpoint with its own signature and the primary's signed proposal. A fenced primary can also seek a replacement standby under the ranked policy. Neither path requires a witness shared by all possible grant issuers. The reservation uses the local counter plus a fixed reserve, bounded by the number format. Successors with equal inherited floors and local high-water marks can derive the same fresh reservation even when their signing keys and standby keys differ. The allocator enforces the range arithmetic. The standby authenticates and sequences the proposal and persists its value. Its receipt supplies local acceptance and carries no independent proof that this range is disjoint from another valid issuer's range. [Standby assignment and replacement](../../box/src/link/setup/handover.ts#L102-L111) · [Reservation calculation](../../box/src/link/setup/handover.ts#L150-L187) · [Proposed standby acceptance](../../box/src/link/setup/leadership.ts#L78-L93) · [Allocation gate](../../box/src/link/work-queue/numbers.ts#L555-L589)

Reconnection orders verified histories by epoch and rank and carries verified revocations forward. The protocol allows ordinary losing-branch edits to be discarded while local work stays with its owners. Neither the reconciliation path nor revocation union proves that a number already returned to a caller remains unique. The audit's partial numbering rating stands. This conclusion concerns delegated numbering and leaves legacy setup-free numbering outside the proof. ## Design decision

**When must a new number wait?** May an item get a fresh setup-wide number as soon as its locally accepted ranked primary has a standby, a live lease and an accepted save, or must it wait until the setup can establish that its range excludes every other valid issuer?

Require the latter before showing a new number or returning a new grant. Keep items usable without a number and let computers spend the unspent part of blocks already durably granted exclusively to them. Once those blocks run out, wait for a setup-wide allocation decision that fences prior issuers and preserves the high-water mark of all issuable reservations. A serialized allocation ledger with intersecting write quorums is one way to supply that evidence. A scheme that preassigns provably disjoint ranges could also permit disconnected issuance within those ranges. A displayed number should remain reserved even if its issuing history later loses. No production behavior changes in this audit.

## One-ledger checks

The unit fixture exercises the managed allocator against one persisted ledger and uses the production save validator to obtain a signed receipt from an in-memory standby. It checks retained grant ID replay, the allocator's use of a takeover counter seeded above the inherited ceiling, local and reserved range exhaustion, a missing primary lease, and withholding a grant until its standby receipt is installed durably.
